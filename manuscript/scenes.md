# Scenes

Chapter briefs, v1.0 (2026-11-18). The story runs from the princes'
childhood to the flight home, in 18 chapters. Canon and brief rules:
`README.md`. **Bold** = identifiable character (`characters.md`);
everyone else is a generic figure.

v0.5 replaces the panel-by-panel scene lines (v0.4) with one **brief**
per chapter: the chapter's visual narrative in a handful of beats. The
panel side (`panels/`) breaks beats into panels and owns panel ids,
sizes, shots and order; the manuscript owns the story, the cast, the
chapter boundaries and the wordless test. **Chapter 5 is the only
artist-reviewed chapter and the model for the rest**: the panels drawn
for chapters 1–4 and 6–10 are to be revisited against these briefs —
they are too complex and do not carry the narrative clearly yet
(artist, 2026-11-07). v0.8 drops the fire god from chapter 18: Sita
walks into the fire and walks out of it — no deity on panel. v0.9
drops Ravana's chariot (5–6): he flies as he is, holding Sita. v1.0
swaps chapters 15 and 16 into the popular order — the mountain of herbs
(15) before Indrajit's death (16), Indrajit's javelin felling Lakshman
— and brings briefs 1–14 in line with the panels drawn so far (the
panels win where they disagree).

A **beat** is one movement of the story, told in one or a few bullets,
each a gesture or action that works as a wordless image. A chapter can
more or less hope to cover **2–3 beats** and no more (artist,
2026-11-08). v0.6 applied this to chapters 6–8 (6 trimmed, the old 7
split into 7 "Sugriva" and 8 "Bali"); v0.7 to the whole book: the old
9 split into 9 "The leap" and 10 "The ring", old 10–17 renumbered
11–18, and every remaining chapter trimmed to the rule. A beat may become one panel or several
(establishing a new place and its key figure is always a sequence, not
one panel — see chapter 5). Details that do not move the main
narrative are left out; the beats are the story, not an inventory.

## Continuity and motifs

The panel writer works one chapter at a time; these are the threads
that must stay consistent across chapters.

**Places that return.** Every chapter opens with a location panel;
when the story comes back to a place, the same image returns.

| Place | Image | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Ayodhya | the palace on the horizon | opens 1 |
| The hut | the forest hut from above | 3 (the home image), opens 5's forest half, empty in 6 |
| Lanka, far | the island across the sea | opens 5 and 12 |
| Kishkindha | rocky hills on the horizon | opens 7 and 9; 8 stands before its gates, near |
| The ashoka grove | **Sita** under a tree, rakshasis guarding her | first seen in 10, when Hanuman finds her — the reader discovers it with him; wrecked in 11 |
| The Lanka throne | **Ravana** enthroned (the gold-backed Lanka throne, never Ayodhya's) | 5, 11, 12, 14; **Vibhishana** crowned at it, 18 |
| Lanka's ledge | the rock edge of the island over the sea | Hanuman leaves from it (11); the rakshasas see the army, and the monkeys land on it (13) |

**Objects that travel.** Drawn the same every time they appear:

- **The bundle of jewels** (the `bag` prop): let go by Sita in the air
  (6), picked up by **Hanuman** among the monkeys (6), held out by him
  to **Rama** (7).
- **Rama's ring**: given to **Hanuman** (9), given by him to **Sita** (10)
  — the one token; nothing travels back.
- **Rama's bow**: shot at the rakshasas Surpanakha brings (4), drawn
  after the deer (5), shot from hiding (8),
  drawn at the ocean itself (12), bent upward at the giant (14), bent
  full for the last arrow (17), laid down at Ravana's pyre (17).

**Mirrors.** Compositions that answer each other; let them rhyme:

- Banished by pointing: **Kaikeyi** points **Rama** away (2);
  **Ravana** points **Vibhishana** away (12).
- The exile's place: **Rama** facing us, Ayodhya's palace behind him
  (2); **Sugriva** on his rock, Kishkindha on its hill beyond — the
  city he is shut out of (7).
- Two coronations: **Sugriva** crowned at Kishkindha's throne (8),
  **Vibhishana** at Lanka's (18) — the same crown, each king at his
  own throne.
- Over the sea: **Hanuman** crosses it out in the leap (9), the army walks it on the bridge (12), and
  **Sita** flies home beside **Rama** (18) — her crossing away is
  never shown.
- Hopeless before the sea: the monkeys at the shore before Hanuman's
  leap (9) and again before the bridge (12) — first one crosses alone,
  then everyone crosses together.
- A pointed direction is the story's only "word": Surpanakha points
  the rakshasas at the brothers (4), Sugriva points to the sky Sita
  crossed (7), a guard points
  Ravana back to the wrecked grove (11), the Ocean points across the
  water (12), the healer points north (15).
- Pointed away: Kaikeyi points Rama out (2), Ravana points
  Vibhishana out (12) and Indrajit out to fight (11).
- The travelling cry: Rama's cry crosses the forest to the hut (5), and
  Sita's reaches Jatayu on his bluff (6).
- Negative (white on black) for a transformation or a great death: the
  deer struck and the ascetic's disguise thrown off (5), the giant cut
  apart (14), Indrajit's head (16).
- Fire turns the story: the pact sworn over fire (7), Hanuman's
  burning tail (11), Indrajit's rite (16), Ravana's pyre (17), Sita's
  trial (18).

**The wordless test** (binding; artist, 2026-11-07): no orders,
refusals, bargains or explanations. No creature turns into another on
panel — a disguise thrown off is fine (the ascetic rears up into
Ravana), a transformation is not (the dying deer stays a deer). A beat
that needs words to be understood is cut, or found a gesture.

## 1. Intro

Ayodhya and its court on the day Rama is to be crowned — and Kaikeyi
apart from the joy.

- The palace of Ayodhya on the horizon, open ground before it.
- **Dasharatha** on his throne; a herald strides past, pointing on
  ahead.
- **Rama**, **Sita** and **Lakshman** walk in procession, a horn player
  and a drummer behind them.
- The king leans back from his throne, reaching toward Rama;
  **Kausalya** reaches after the king; **Kaikeyi** stands behind, hand
  on hip.
- Kaikeyi, close, shouting; a storm cloud in her thought bubble.

Must show: the procession and the king's reach as the coronation day —
the crown is promised, never given; Kaikeyi standing apart, her storm
cloud is chapter 2 arriving. Not shown: the bow contest (dropped); the
growing-up ladder and the wedding of the earlier briefs — the artist's
court drafts (2026-11-14) begin at the coronation (see Dropped, v1.0).

## 2. The throne

Kaikeyi's anger wins: Rama is pointed away from the throne, and the
three walk out of the kingdom.

- The Ayodhya throne, empty.
- **Kaikeyi** rages at **Dasharatha**: she shouts, she lies on the
  floor in anger; the king kneels to her, palms joined, and she turns
  away (variations; the artist picks).
- Kaikeyi points **Rama** away, the king on the throne behind; Rama
  takes it in silence, head low, and walks away alone.
- Rama faces us, the palace behind him; **Lakshman** and **Sita**
  hurry after him; the three walk on — past a river and a boat, into
  the trees.

Must show: the pointing-away (it returns when Ravana banishes
**Vibhishana**, 12); the king begging and refused — the boons are told
by posture; Rama's silence; the palace behind him as he faces us. Not
shown: the boons and the bargain (wordless); Dasharatha's death,
Bharat and the sandals stay dropped.

## 3. The forest

Quiet years: the walk in, the lake, the deer, the friend Jatayu, the
hut — and at the end, someone watching.

- The three walk into the forest: over a rising fold of ground,
  **Sita** bending to a flower; running through the trees after a
  leaping stag; small, past a lotus pond.
- Sita points at a stag and two does; **Rama** stands beside her.
- The vulture Jatayu on a rock; Rama and **Lakshman** look up at him,
  Sita reaches up to him.
- The hut from above, green ground around it: the chapter's home
  image; the brothers walk to it, Sita kneels to a deer.
- **Surpanakha** watches the three, then Rama alone, from behind a
  tree; close, a heart in her thought bubble.

Must show: Jatayu befriended (he must be recognised when he fights in
6); Surpanakha's watching closes the quiet. Not shown: the sage
teaching the three (not drawn; dropped); the years passing (the season
icons, dropped 2026-11-18).

## 4. Surpanakha

A stranger's advance turns to violence, and the rakshasas she brings
are destroyed: the first blood of the story.

- **Surpanakha** walks past the trees; she reaches for **Rama**, who
  turns away, and presses close as he steps back; Rama points her to
  **Lakshman**, who leans away; the brothers point her back and forth,
  she between them; close, she reaches for Lakshman.
- Lakshman swings his sword; she clutches her face; close, a hand to
  her face; she screams, front view, and runs off, arms in the air.
- She comes back pointing ahead, armed rakshasas running in behind her;
  the brothers see them coming and both shoot; the rakshasas are struck
  and fall, the rest flee, weapons thrown away; Surpanakha stands
  among the fallen, a hand to her face.

Must show: **Surpanakha** is a rakshasi from her first appearance (the
`surpanakha` character — no fair form, no transformation); her pointing
the rakshasas at the brothers — she brings the war down on them, and
carries the wound on to Lanka in 5; Lakshman fights beside Rama. Not
shown: Khara (the rakshasas are generic); Sita — she stays out of the
chapter, so there is no lunge at her and no embrace at the end.

## 5. The golden deer

Lanka reaches into the forest: the deer draws Rama away, the cry draws
Lakshman away, and Ravana takes Sita. The hinge of the story.
(Artist-reviewed; its drawn panels are the model chapter.)

- Lanka on its island, far across the sea, then closer: first sight of
  the enemy's home.
- **Surpanakha** runs up the palace steps, crying out; the Lanka
  throne, far, then close: **Ravana** the king.
- Surpanakha comes before the throne, arms up, **Vibhishana** at
  Ravana's side; she bows, her hands to her face; Ravana steps down;
  she pleads, and he shouts.
- Back in the forest: the hut from above, all quiet.
- **Sita** sees a golden deer far off on the horizon and points;
  **Rama** chases it, far off; he draws; in negative, the arrow strikes
  it; the deer sinks, head down.
- A cry goes up with Rama's shouting face and travels across the
  forest to the hut: Sita and **Lakshman** look up; she points him
  away, and he goes.
- Lakshman runs toward the cry; an ascetic watches from behind; the
  hut alone; the ascetic at a distance raises his begging bowl; Sita
  offers him fruit; he lunges; in negative, the disguise falls away
  and it is Ravana who has her by the arm; he lifts her off her feet.
- **Ravana** rises into the sky with **Sita**, sword in hand; she
  reaches out and down.

Must show: the deer stays far away and is never revealed as anything
else; the cry as a travelling image; negative —
white on black — for the deer struck and the disguise thrown off
(negative is the picture for a transformation and for a major death);
Ravana flying off by himself, holding her — nothing carries them. Not
shown: Ravana ordering Maricha, Maricha refusing, the deer's true form
— cut by the wordless test; the deer simply appears, and simply dies;
Ravana's chariot (artist, 2026-11-08) — there is no chariot in the
abduction or in Jatayu's fight.

## 6. Jatayu

The abduction witnessed: the old vulture gives his life, the monkeys
see the jewels fall, and Rama comes home to emptiness.

- Jatayu on his bluff; Sita's cry reaches him from far off — the cry
  travels on from 5; he takes off toward it, comes at the flying
  **Ravana**, swoops, talons out; Ravana's sword cuts off a wing;
  Jatayu tumbles out of the sky; Ravana flies on with **Sita**.
- High in the sky Sita holds out the bundle of jewels and lets it go;
  it falls alone; it lands on the rocks among the monkeys, **Hanuman**
  bending to it; then they look up at the pair passing small overhead.
- The hut from above, empty; **Lakshman** runs back alone; **Rama**
  flings his arms out; they search among the trees, apart, back at
  the hut; Rama kneels at the ground, Lakshman points ahead; far off,
  the cut wing; Rama kneels, reaching to the dying Jatayu sprawled on
  the ground.

Must show: the bundle (the `bag` prop) falling into monkey hands — it
surfaces in 7; the order the artist chose: she drops it, it falls, it
lands and is picked up, and only then do the monkeys look up; Rama
kneeling to Jatayu, the friend of 3 — the last of him. Not
shown: the crossing of the sea and Sita's arrival in the grove (the
grove is first seen when Hanuman finds it in 10); the funeral pyre —
Rama's reach to the dying bird is Jatayu's last image.

## 7. Sugriva

Two exiles meet: grief answers grief, and a pact is sworn over fire.

- The rocky hills: **Hanuman** crouches on a near slope; **Rama** and
  **Lakshman** walk below a high rock; Hanuman and a monkey peer down
  from its edge; Hanuman leads them over the hill.
- Up to **Sugriva** on his rock, Kishkindha on its hill beyond — a king
  camped outside his own city; Hanuman kneels and holds out the bundle
  of jewels; Rama holds it; Sugriva points to the sky she was carried
  across; Rama grieves on the rock.
- On the rock, Kishkindha beyond, Rama steps toward Sugriva; Sugriva
  bows his head, a hand to his face — his own grief; they sit face to
  face, each on a round rock; by night they clasp hands over a fire.

Must show: the jewels are the bundle dropped in 6. The agreement is
an exchange of griefs, never spoken: the bundle held out is Rama's loss,
Sugriva's bowed head with Kishkindha beyond him is his (the city he is
shut out of); the fire between them is the witness, and each half is
paid on panel — the
hidden arrow in 8, the search sent out in 9. Sugriva's side needs no
backstory: his exile is told by where he sits, on the rock outside the
walls. Not shown: Hanuman carrying the princes on his shoulders (a
flourish that hides the figures); Bali's old wrong against Sugriva
(backstory needs words — the gaze at the shut city carries it).

## 8. Bali

The pact paid: Sugriva roars his challenge, the brothers fight, one
hidden arrow decides it, and the crown changes heads.

- Hanuman and **Rama** on the rock, **Sugriva** crouched below,
  Kishkindha beyond. Sugriva runs up the palace steps roaring; Bali
  comes out at the door, charges down and flings him back on the
  steps; Rama runs past below the hill, bow in hand.
- The brothers fight — flying at each other, tumbling, grappling;
  Rama watches from behind the trees, kneels and draws; the arrow
  loosed; Bali is flung back and falls with the arrow in him; Sugriva
  throws up his arms.
- Musicians and leaping monkeys lead **Sugriva** in procession; he
  sits crowned on Kishkindha's own throne, **Hanuman** and Angad
  beside him.

Must show: the challenge as pure posture at the palace steps — mouth
wide, no parley; Rama shooting from hiding — his half of the pact over the fire in 7; Kishkindha's throne, not the gold-backed Lanka throne,
which stays Ravana's until Vibhishana is crowned at it in 18 — the
coronation image returns there; Tara and Angad are generic monkeys —
only Hanuman and Sugriva are named; Bali is Sugriva's double (same
body, same cloth), and in the fights Sugriva wears a flower garland
flying back from his neck, the one mark that tells the brothers apart.
Not shown: Tara holding Bali back (a warning needs words); the
mourning over Bali (not drawn).

## 9. The leap

The search sent out narrows to one monkey, and the sea to one leap.

- Kishkindha on its hill again; **Rama** gives **Hanuman** his ring —
  close, it falls from Rama's hand into his; **Sugriva** sends monkeys
  out in four directions.
- The search travels: a bare slope, a great rock, ferns under hanging
  vines, a dry plain, emptier — one landscape per panel, the figures
  small — until, from above, the land runs out and the monkeys stand
  at the water's edge.
- **Hanuman** stands on the summit over the sea, a monkey below
  pointing up at him; he crouches, and leaps — close, then across the
  whole sky, the ocean far below.

Must show: the ring passing from Rama's hand to Hanuman's (it reaches
Sita in 10); the search as a run of landscapes, the world growing
emptier until only water is left; the water's edge from above (it
returns, the whole army this time, in 12); the leap using the sky's
full height.

## 10. The ring

Hanuman alone in the enemy's city: Sita found, and Rama's ring
given.

- The leap carries on over the ocean; Lanka, closer.
- Night: **Hanuman**, small, clings to a tall palace wall and peers
  into its lit windows; he scurries over the roofs, leans out from
  their edges, looks across the sleeping city — one place per panel,
  Hanuman small against each — and **Sita** is nowhere; he is at a
  loss in the dark.
- The ashoka grove: Sita by the tree between two rakshasis, Hanuman
  crouched above — the reader finds her with him; Sita alone on her
  rock, head bowed.
- **Ravana** comes to the grove and stands over her; he leans in,
  coaxing, and she fends him off; he strides away; rakshasis close in,
  shouting, and a kind one drives another off; night, then dawn, Sita
  alone (variations; the artist picks).
- By day Hanuman drops down before her and kneels, reaching up; close,
  Rama's ring passes from his hand into hers; he bows, she joins her
  palms.

Must show: the night search as a run of places without her — like the
landscapes in 9 — so the grove lands as a discovery; the grove image
exactly as it will be wrecked in 11; the ring passing from Hanuman's
hand into Sita's — the ring that left Rama's hand in 9; Sita on the same
rock in the same spot throughout. The kind rakshasi is generic
(`raxas_f3`), not named. Not shown: Sita's hair jewel — only the ring
passes (2026-11-18).

## 11. Lanka burns

Hanuman caught becomes Hanuman the torch: he leaves Lanka burning
behind him.

- On the ledge at the edge of Lanka, the sea before him, **Hanuman**
  turns back toward the city, an explosion in his thought bubble, and
  runs back in; he heaves trees over in the grove, holds a felled
  trunk high, and the guards run.
- A guard runs to **Ravana**'s throne, pointing back the way he came;
  Ravana rises in rage and points; **Indrajit** bows and runs out with
  his bow drawn; he aims up at Hanuman swinging on a vine; the
  arrow-ropes coil round Hanuman, and he is brought bound before the
  throne.
- Rakshasas set his tail on fire; the ropes burst; he leaps over the
  roofs, tail aflame; the palace burns and rakshasas flee through the
  streets; on the shore he dips his tail in the sea, Lanka burning
  across the water.

Must show: the turn back before the wrecking — the havoc is his own
idea, told by the thought bubble, not an order; **Indrajit**
introduced as the binder, sent by his father's pointing arm (his own
chapter is 16); the burning leap is the chapter's height image. Not shown: Vibhishana pleading for Hanuman's
life (a plea for mercy needs words; Vibhishana's story starts in 12).

## 12. The bridge

Hanuman is back, the army marches, Lanka divides, and the sea is
crossed: one rakshasa flies over alone, then a whole army walks over
on stone.

- At Kishkindha's throne **Hanuman** stands before **Rama** and
  **Lakshman**, arms up — she is found; **Sugriva** points the army
  on, and the monkeys march out, swords raised.
- At the Lanka throne, **Vibhishana** kneels, palms joined, pleading;
  **Ravana** stands and points him away — as Kaikeyi pointed Rama;
  Vibhishana turns away, head bowed, and flies across the sea alone;
  on **Rama**'s shore he kneels, and Rama reaches down to raise him.
- From above, the army at the water's edge, as in 9; the monkeys sit,
  heads bowed before the water; Rama draws his bow at the ocean — and
  the Ocean rises from the waves before the drawn bow, palms raised,
  and points across the water; Rama lowers his bow.
- Monkeys hurry down with rocks held overhead and hurl them into the
  sea; the line of stone grows toward Lanka on the horizon; the army
  crosses on it, **Rama** walking behind **Hanuman**, **Lakshman** and
  Vibhishana after them — Lanka, far for so long, stands near.

Must show: the pointing-away mirror (Kaikeyi, 2); the water's edge as
in 9 — there one monkey leapt alone, now everyone crosses together;
the bridge growing as a line of stone toward the Lanka of the opener.
Not shown: anyone carried over the bridge — riding on shoulders is not
drawn (artist, 2026-11-17); a named builder (Nala stays a generic
monkey); the squirrel helping (a later telling).

## 13. The war begins

The first battle: the walls stormed, the serpent arrows fail, and
Lanka's champions fall one after another — the war starts going
against Ravana.

- Lanka across the sea; on Lanka's ledge, rakshasas point out to sea,
  alarmed; on the same ledge the monkeys leap up out of the sea and
  run in — the two sides never yet in one panel.
- The siege: rakshasas line the wall with spears, swords and clubs,
  and hurl rocks down; monkeys carry ladders to the wall and swarm up,
  ram it with felled trunks, hurl rocks back; climbers fall, a ladder
  is pushed off, defenders fall from the wall top.
- From a cloud, **Indrajit**'s serpent arrows bind **Rama** and
  **Lakshman**; they fall — until the serpents loosen, slither away,
  and the brothers rise (to be drawn).
- The battle turns: rakshasa chiefs come out one after another and
  fall — one lunges at **Hanuman**'s lifted boulder, one falls head
  first from the wall; the field before Lanka strewn with the fallen
  and the thrown rocks.

Must show: the mounting deaths on Ravana's side — they are why the
giant is woken in 14; the fallen field is the chapter's last image;
Indrajit's cloud (it returns in 15, and its source, the fire, in 16).
Not shown: Garuda freeing the brothers (a second great bird reads as
Jatayu back from the dead — the serpents simply slither away); Rama
shooting away Ravana's crown and bow, and the bare-headed walk (a
detail too fine for wordless panels); Sita shown the fallen brothers
(a side scene); Lakshman felled here (he falls once, in 15).

## 14. Kumbhakarna

The giant: waking him is a comedy, meeting him is a terror; Rama cuts
him down piece by piece.

- Lanka across the sea; **Ravana** at his throne sends out the
  wakers.
- The giant Kumbhakarna asleep, larger than the panel; tiny rakshasas
  walk in past his upturned sole with drum and horn; one hauls at his
  hanging hand; drums and horn blast at his head; a steaming bowl, a
  heap of food by his mouth; his eye, still shut — then it opens, and
  the yawn sends them running.
- The monkeys flee under his reaching fingers; his face rises over
  Lanka's wall; his foot comes down among them; **Hanuman** heaves a
  mountain peak up at him — it breaks on the giant's fist.
- **Rama** aims high at the arrow-stuck face; the arrows cut him apart
  — the grabbing hand, the fist, the foot fall away whole, in
  negative; the last arrow, and the great head falls (variations: into
  the sea, or on the shore with the monkeys dancing beside it).

Must show: scale — the giant only ever as his parts (face, hand, fist,
foot, waist) against whole small figures; the waking ladder (drums,
food, the steaming bowl) as one rising joke; the cutting apart in
negative, a major death. Not shown: Kumbhakarna before Ravana's throne
(counsel needs words); Sugriva seized and escaping (a side struggle).

## 15. The mountain of herbs

Indrajit's javelin fells Lakshman; Hanuman flies north and, rather
than choose a herb, brings back the whole mountain.

- Lanka by night. **Indrajit**, from his cloud as in 13, hurls a
  javelin; it strikes **Lakshman** down; **Rama** kneels over him,
  head bowed; an old monkey healer points north, **Hanuman** beside
  him looking where he points.
- Hanuman leaps north over the sea and over the land by night, the
  white mountains ahead; on the mountain of glowing herbs he looks
  about and scratches his head — then lifts the whole mountain
  overhead, and flies back through the night with it on one palm, the
  sea below.
- The healer holds a herb to Lakshman's face; Lakshman rises; the
  brothers embrace.

Must show: the unseen archer's cloud (the source of it, his fire, is
shown in 16, where losing it is losing him); the pointed direction
(north, as the Ocean pointed across the water in 12); the head-scratch before the lift —
the war's one joke; the night flight is the height image. Not shown:
Rama holding Lakshman in his lap (no pose for it yet — he kneels over
him); the herbs hiding themselves from Hanuman (Valmiki's reason for
taking the peak needs words; the head-scratch carries the popular
one); the healer fetched from Lanka (in the popular telling he is
Lanka's physician; here he is a monkey, as in Valmiki).

## 16. Indrajit

The unseen archer: his power is his fire rite; caught at it, he falls
to the Lakshman he felled.

- **Indrajit** pours offerings into a sacrificial fire in his shrine
  at Nikumbhila — the fire his cloud comes from.
- **Vibhishana** leads **Lakshman** and monkeys through Lanka to the
  shrine; Indrajit is caught at his rite and leaves the fire.
- The duel: Indrajit rises into the sky and shoots down, Lakshman
  shoots from the ground; arrows cross; Lakshman breaks his bow; the
  last arrow strikes off his head (in negative, as the giant's in 14).

Must show: the fire rite before the fight (his invisibility has a
visible source, and losing the fire is losing it); Vibhishana leading
the way — he knows Lanka; Lakshman, risen in 15, the one who ends him.
Not shown: the false Sita killed before the army — an illusion reads
as the real Sita or as a stranger, and either needs words; Ravana's
grief at the news — the next chapter opens with him striding out.

## 17. Ravana's fall

The last duel, face to face on the field: heads regrow until the one
great arrow finds his chest, and the bow is laid down.

- **Ravana**, his son dead, strides out of Lanka, arms full of
  weapons; **Rama** stands to meet him; arrows cross.
- **Rama**'s arrow cuts off a head; a new head grows in its place.
- Rama bends his bow full with one great arrow; it pierces
  **Ravana**'s chest; he falls. **Vibhishana** lights the pyre; Rama
  lays down his bow.

Must show: the regrowing head as a visible loop (cut off, grown back —
why only the one great arrow, aimed at the chest, can end him); the
laid-down bow ends the war. Not shown: the ten-headed form — Ravana
keeps his drawn look throughout the story; Ravana's javelin felling
Lakshman (it is Indrajit's, in 15); Indra's chariot and its driver

## 18. Return

Fire gives Sita back, and the swan chariot carries them home — waving
to the reader.

- The crowned **Vibhishana** at the Lanka throne — as Sugriva stood at
  his.
- **Sita** stands before **Rama**; he looks away; she steps into the
  fire.
- **Sita** walks out of the flames unharmed; **Rama** embraces her.
- The swan-drawn chariot flies them home; **Rama** and **Sita** wave
  back at the reader.
- The book closes on a thin panel: ॥ इति ॥ in orange, nothing else —
  the colophon, not a scene.

Must show: the coronation mirror (the same crown, each king at his own
throne — the Lanka throne here, whichever way the artist draws it);
the fire trial as two panels, in and out — the same fire, Sita unharmed;
Sita crossing the sea again, forward this time; the wave to the reader
is the story's last image, sealed by the ॥ इति ॥ strip. Not shown:
the fire god rising with her in his arms — the flames give her back by
themselves: she walks in and she walks out; Ayodhya's return — the
story ends on the flight itself.

**The story ends here.**
