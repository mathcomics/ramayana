# Sequential grammar (the clear-line school)

Principles of wordless sequential storytelling, distilled from the
clear-line tradition (Hergé is its master study) and adapted to this
project's vertical scroll. **Principles only, never drawings**: the repo
rule stands — no tracing or copying third-party art; our forms come from
the artist's own work and the style bible. Read with
`panel-writing.md` (the checklist) and `composition.md` (arrangement).

## Camera discipline

- One steady eye-level camera is the default; it only moves when the
  story needs it (a zoom step for attention, a top view for geography, a
  low horizon for distance). An angle change is an event — spend it.
- Consecutive panels of one scene keep the camera on the same side of
  the actors (never jump the line): a figure facing right keeps facing
  right until the story turns them.

## Cause and effect across the gutter

- Each panel answers the one before: gesture → consequence, look →
  what is seen, cry → who hears it. If a panel's connection to the
  previous one needs explaining, a beat is missing between them (a
  location panel, a glance, a partial view).
- Repetition with one small change is the clock of the story: the same
  walking figures across three panels is a journey; the
  same bubble across three panels is a sound traveling (05.13-05.15).
  Keep everything else identical so the change carries the meaning.

## Readability

- One action per panel, readable from the silhouette alone; faces only
  refine what the body already says.
- The background serves the action: empty ground during gestures and
  dialogue, scenery only where the story looks at it. A detail that does
  not serve the panel's one action is noise.
- Figure proportions never exaggerate for emotion; scale encodes only
  distance (and a deliberate menace step, e.g. Ravana 1.1-1.15).

## Direction grammar (proposed — artist to confirm)

The clear-line convention: movement **left to right** is outward,
onward, into the unknown; **right to left** is return, retreat, home.
Applied here it would mean: the exile sets out rightward, the return to
Ayodhya flows leftward, and antagonists enter against the heroes'
direction. Today's chapters are mixed (the exile walks left
in chapter 2; the deer chase runs right). Adopting the rule means
re-facing some existing panels — **not applied yet**; if the artist
confirms, apply it chapter by chapter and note it in `composition.md`.

## For the vertical scroll in particular

- The scroll gutter is time: a tall panel is a held breath, a run of 0.6
  strips is quick cutting. Vary panel height with the story's pulse, not
  by habit (the height rule in panel-writing.md).
- A reveal lands after a scroll beat: put the quiet panel (location,
  empty ground) immediately before the surprise, so the reader scrolls
  into it (05.08 quiet hut → the deer appears).
